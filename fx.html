Good to know

Reading these numbers

What is the official rate?

The volume-weighted average rate from the Nigerian Foreign Exchange Market (NFEM). The rate the CBN recognises for the day. It updates once per business day, so Friday's rate carries through the weekend.

What is the parallel market rate?

The street rate: what dealers outside the official market actually trade at. It moves through the day and differs by dealer and city, so treat our number as indicative, not a quote you can trade on.

Why are the two different?

The official rate reflects transactions inside the formal market; the street rate reflects supply and demand outside it. When demand for foreign currency outruns official supply, the street rate trades higher. On 16 October 2026 the dollar gap was ₦29.27 (2.20%).

How fresh is this data?

The official rate refreshes once daily; the street rate is pulled once a day. Each card shows exactly when its number was last updated.

Buy vs sell: what do the two prices mean?

A dealer's buy price is what they pay you for your foreign currency; the sell price is what you pay them. The difference is their margin. Always confirm which side of the quote you are on.

Which rate do money transfer apps use?

Apps like LemFi, Grey and Sendwave price near the parallel rate, not the CBN rate, then add their fee or margin on top. Compare by what the recipient actually receives, not the headline rate.

Is buying foreign currency on the street legal?

Buying foreign currency for personal use is not a crime, but unlicensed dealing in bulk is. For larger amounts, use a licensed BDC or your bank, and keep receipts.

Rates are for information only, not investment advice. The parallel market rate is unofficial and indicative. Confirm actual rates with your dealer before acting.

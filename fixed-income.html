What the stop rate means for you

Stop rate. The highest yield the CBN accepted at the auction. Every successful bidder gets this rate, so it is the market price of that tenor.

Discount pricing. You do not pay the full face value. You pay less now and receive the full amount at maturity; the difference is your interest. At 15.90% for 364 days, ₦1,000,000 buys ₦1,188,445 of face value.

Subscription vs allotment. Subscription is how much investors bid; allotment is how much the CBN actually sold. When subscription dwarfs allotment, demand is hot and the next stop rate often falls.

Annualised. The rate scaled to a full year, so a 91-day and a 364-day rate can be compared fairly.

Bullet repayment. Savings bonds repay the full principal in one lump at maturity, while interest arrives quarterly.

How to buy

T-bills, primary auction. Place your bid through a bank, stockbroker, or fintech app before the auction closes on Wednesday morning. Fund the account, state the tenor and amount, and the CBN allots at the stop rate. Your account is debited the discounted price; the full face value lands automatically at maturity.

T-bills, secondary market. Buy an existing bill from a bank or broker before it matures. The price moves with current rates, so you can enter or exit between auctions.

Minimums depend on the channel. Direct bids at the CBN auction start around ₦50m, but banks pool smaller retail orders from about ₦50,000, and fintech apps go from about ₦10,000. Confirm the figure with your provider before you commit.

Savings bonds. Subscribe through any DMO-appointed stockbroker during the monthly 5-day window. Minimum ₦5,000, maximum ₦50m. Coupons arrive quarterly; the full principal returns at maturity.

Thinking about stocks instead? T-bills are the yardstick: with the 364-day at 15.85% against inflation at 15.39%, equities need to beat roughly 16% to justify their risk. Falling T-bill yields make shares look more attractive; rising yields do the opposite.

The premium trap: read this before buying any ETF

An ETF's market price and the value of what it holds (its net asset value, or NAV) are two different numbers. When buyers pile in faster than new units are created, the price detaches above NAV: that gap is the premium. When sellers dominate, it trades at a discount.

Nigeria saw this up close in early 2025: index-tracking ETFs soared to triple-digit premiums as retail demand overwhelmed supply, then fell by half in weeks when prices snapped back toward NAV. Buyers at the top paid two or three times the underlying value, and the ETF itself did nothing wrong.

The practical rule: check the premium or discount before every buy. The fund manager publishes the NAV, usually daily on their website. Compare it with the last traded price here. A small premium is normal. A large one is a warning.

Frequently asked questions

What is an ETF?

An exchange-traded fund is a basket of assets (stocks, bonds or gold) that trades on the stock exchange like a single share. You buy it through any stockbroker, exactly like a stock.

How do I buy an ETF in Nigeria?

Open an account with an NGX-licensed stockbroker, fund it, and place a buy order using the ETF's symbol (for example STANBICETF30). Settlement and fees work like ordinary shares.

Do NGX ETFs pay dividends?

Some distribute income from their holdings; others reinvest it. Check each fund's page and the manager's distribution notices.

ETF or mutual fund: what's the difference?

Both pool your money into a basket of assets. The difference is the door you use: an ETF trades live on the exchange through your stockbroker, with prices you can see all session; a mutual fund is bought from the manager and priced once a day. Similar baskets, different doors.

How much do I need to start?

Whatever one unit costs, plus your broker's fees. There is no fixed minimum. A unit of VETBANK costs less than lunch; a unit of NewGold costs as much as a used car. Start where your pocket allows.

Are these prices live?

They are daily closes from NGX ETF data, refreshed every trading day, not intraday ticks. Good enough for comparing funds; confirm the current price with your broker before placing an order.
